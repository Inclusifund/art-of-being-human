The Art of Being Human

Egypt · November 18–28, 2026

A 10-day journey into what it means to be human.

02 — The Invitation

What if you didn’t have to become anything?

What if you could simply arrive? With your light. Your shadow. Your questions. Your contradictions. Your pleasure. Your grief. Your laughter.

What if there was nothing to fix? Only something to experience.

An invitation to slow down, feel deeply and meet yourself exactly where you are.

03 — Why Egypt

Why Egypt?

Some places you visit. Some places you feel. Egypt is one of them.

Ancient. Alive. Contrasting. Impossible to explain completely.

Stone and water. Desert and sea. Past and present. We begin here.

04 — The Journey

Three days in the desert.

The world becomes quieter. We walk. We sleep in a cave. We gather around the fire.

We let the desert do what it does. Strip away what isn't necessary.

Nothing to prove. Nowhere to rush. Just here.

05 — The Desert Ceremony

And then, we go inward.

As the afternoon unfolds, we enter a carefully held desert ceremony. The vastness of the landscape. The silence. The setting sun.

Space for a deeper encounter with ourselves.

Nothing to force. Nothing to perform. Whatever is ready to be met, we meet.

06 — Integration

Return to the body.

At Malakot, we soften. We rebirth in the temazcal, the mother's womb.

Heat. Cold. Water. Flowers. Fire. Breath.

We let the experience settle. We integrate. We come back.

07 — The Red Sea

Then, the land disappears.

The Red Sea opens. We sail. We swim. We float. We laugh. We rest.

And somewhere between the water and the sky, time begins to disappear.

08 — The Full Moon

The full moon rises.

A past-life regression ceremony, held on the water. We listen to what lies beneath the story we know.

Then we look forward. What do we want to create?

09 — The Blue Lotus Ceremony

The following evening.

A Blue Lotus ceremony, inspired by ancient Egyptian symbolism.

The threshold between waking and dreaming. A quiet space for inner listening, imagination, and the mysteries of the night.

10 — The Final Sunset

And then, sunset.

Our last evening at sea. Cacao. Fresh fruit. Music. The last light.

And we dance. Not because we have somewhere to go. But because we are here. Alive. Together.

An exhale.

11 — The Experience

What it feels like to be there.

Space to breathe. Time in nature. Movement. Connection. Silence. Laughter. Deep conversations. Fire. Water. The unknown.

You don’t need to perform transformation. You simply need to arrive.

Moments of being held. Moments of letting go. Moments that cannot be planned.

12 — Who This Is For

For all of us.

All genders. All orientations. All identities. All ways of being.

There is no particular way you need to be to belong here. Come as you are.

Different stories. Different bodies. Different lives. One human circle.

We are all just being human.

13 — Meet Mehta & Trish

How this began

This is not something I invented. It is something I have lived.

A story about Egypt. Travel. The body. Movement. Connection. Women. Human. Culture. And the questions that keep bringing me deeper into what it means to be human.

Practical

The shape of the ten days

Dates
18–28 November 2026 · ten days
Arrive
Fly into Sharm el-Sheikh (SSH) on 18 November.
Depart
Fly home from Hurghada (HRG) on 28 November.
Getting between them
One internal flight on 22 November, Sharm el-Sheikh to Hurghada, and on from there to the boat at Marsa Alam. Arranged as part of the journey — you do not book it yourself.

Where you will be

  1. 18–21 Nov The Sinai desert. Walking, silence, sleeping in a cave, gathering at the fire.
  2. 21–22 Nov Malakot Oasis. One night. Temazcal, ice bath, flower bath, integration — the day the body catches up with the desert.
  3. 22–26 Nov The Red Sea, aboard MV Legends. You board at Marsa Alam and spend the first night at dock, then sail for Sataya at sunrise. Swimming, the full moon, and the dolphins.
  4. 26–28 Nov The crossing back to Hurghada. From the final sunset the boat turns for home — around thirty hours at sea, and the last of the journey to let it settle.

What to bring

The desert is cold at night and hot by ten in the morning; the boat is warm and wet. Pack for both.

  • Layers for the desert — the temperature drops hard after sunset
  • A warm jacket and something to sleep in
  • Swimwear, and a towel that dries quickly
  • Reef-safe sun cream, a hat, sunglasses
  • Walking shoes and sandals
  • A refillable water bottle
  • Any medication you take, in its original packaging
  • A notebook, if you are someone who writes
  • Loose, comfortable clothes you can move in
